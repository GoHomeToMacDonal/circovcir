# Eleven unit disks, exactly ten of which meet the boundary circle

Notation. For c in the plane, D(c,1) is the closed unit disk centred at c. For R > 0, D_R = D(0,R) is the closed disk of radius R centred at the origin and S_R = {|z| = R} its boundary circle. A disk "meets" S_R if it has at least one point on S_R (tangency counts). Put

    r_0 = 263169/100000 = 2.63169.

**Claim to prove.** Let c_0, c_1, …, c_10 be eleven points of the plane (not necessarily distinct) and R > 0, and suppose

    D_R ⊆ D(c_0,1) ∪ D(c_1,1) ∪ … ∪ D(c_10,1),

and that exactly ten of the eleven disks D(c_i,1) meet S_R. Then R < r_0.

(Context, not part of the claim: the best known covering of a disk by eleven unit disks has radius R_* = 2.6316907726198082… > r_0, and in it exactly nine disks meet S_R; that case is NOT part of this claim. The claim says that coverings in which ten disks meet S_R cannot reach r_0. Numerical searches suggest the supremum of R over this class is (3+√5)/2 = 1 + 2cos(π/5) = 2.6180339887…, attained by one disk centred at the origin and ten disks centred on a regular decagon, but any proof of a bound strictly below r_0 suffices. A proof by hand, by a rigorous finite computation (exact or outward-rounded interval arithmetic), or by a combination is acceptable; any computation must be described precisely enough to be re-run.)

## Known facts that may be used (each with its proof)

F1 (arc bounds). For R > 2, a closed disk of radius s < R meets S_R in an arc of angular measure at most 2·arcsin(s/R). Hence a unit disk covers at most 2a(R) of S_R, a(R) = arcsin(1/R); and any family of unit disks with a common point p lies in D(p,2), so together they cover at most 2d(R) of S_R, d(R) = arcsin(2/R). Both a and d decrease in R.

F2 (structure of the class). Assume R ≥ r_0 > 2 and the hypotheses of the claim. Some disk D(c_i,1) contains the origin, so |c_i| ≤ 1 and every point of it has modulus ≤ 2 < R: it does not meet S_R. Hence it is the unique disk missing S_R; call it D(c_0,1), so |c_0| ≤ 1, and D(c_1,1), …, D(c_10,1) all meet S_R, so |c_i| ≥ R − 1 for i ≥ 1. No disk other than D(c_0,1) contains the origin. (Also π − 8a(r_0) = 0.02336… > 0, so eight unit disks never cover S_R for R ≥ r_0.)

F3 (common-point counting, R ≥ r_0, ten boundary disks). If five of D(c_1,1), …, D(c_10,1) share a common point, the arcs cover at most 2d + 5·2a < 2π of S_R, since π − (d + 5a) > 0.3294 at r_0 — impossible. Likewise three pairwise index-disjoint triples of boundary disks, each triple with a common point, are impossible, since π − (3d + a) > 0.1620 at r_0. These counts do NOT exclude one triple or two index-disjoint triples with common points (2d + 4a − π > 0.1440 at r_0).

F4 (the wheel subclass is excluded). Suppose, in addition, that the eleven centres are distinct and lie in the interior of D_R, and that the nearest-centre (Voronoi) cells V_i = {x ∈ D_R : |x − c_i| ≤ |x − c_j| for all j} have the "ten-spoke wheel" shape: V_0 does not meet S_R and is a convex decagon with vertices p_1, …, p_10 in cyclic order; for i = 1..10 (indices mod 10), V_i is bounded by the three segments p_i p_{i+1}, p_{i+1} q_{i+1}, q_i p_i and the arc q_i q_{i+1} of S_R, p_i being common to V_0, V_{i−1}, V_i; and the arc of V_i subtends an angle α_i ≤ π at c_i. Then R < r_0. Proof (numbers checked by outward-rounded interval arithmetic): covering forces V_i ⊆ D(c_i,1). Split each cell into triangles from its own centre. The decagon and the ten mirror triangles over its edges in the outer cells have total area ≤ Σ sin ε_i ≤ 10 sin(π/5) (ε_i = ∠p_i c_0 p_{i+1}, Σε_i = 2π, sine concave). The twenty triangles c_i p_i q_i, c_i p_{i+1} q_{i+1} have total area ≤ ½Σ(sin β_i + sin γ_i) ≤ 10 sin(9π/10 − ᾱ/2) by Jensen, where Σ(β_i + γ_i) = 18π − Σα_i and ᾱ = Σα_i/10. Each curved region A_i (bounded by c_i q_i, c_i q_{i+1}, of lengths ≤ 1, and the arc q_i q_{i+1}) satisfies A_i ≤ U(α_i), where f(α) = 2 arcsin(sin(α/2)/r_0), F(α) = (r_0²/2)(f − sin f) + ½ sin α, t = 21/10, and U is the tangent line of F at t (F is concave on [π/3, π]; for α < π/3 the region lies in the unit sector at c_i, so A_i ≤ α/2 < U(α); for R ≥ r_0 the circular-segment area at fixed half-chord decreases in R). Summing, with the tangent-line bound for sine, πR² ≤ 10[sin(π/5) + sin(9π/10 − t/2) + F(t) + π|F′(t) − ½cos(9π/10 − t/2)|] = 21.7182352993518… < π r_0² (margin 0.0397827…), a contradiction. This argument does NOT cover configurations whose Voronoi structure is not the ten-spoke wheel — e.g. an outer cell not adjacent to V_0, a Voronoi vertex inside D_R shared by three outer cells, V_0 meeting S_R, some α_i > π, an outer cell meeting S_R in more than one arc — nor coincident centres or centres outside D_R. Those configurations are exactly what remains.
